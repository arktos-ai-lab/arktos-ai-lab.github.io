§ 01 · Paper

Open but unvetted: the ethics of African language data.

Creative Commons licenses are prevalent in African NLP corpus releases, but their compatibility implications are rarely examined systematically. This paper audits the license provenance of 20+ corpus families and documents four structurally distinct ways compliance fails in practice.

§ 01Overview.

NLP researchers are typically not trained in law. For high-resource languages this is rarely a practical problem. Widely used corpora have been informally vetted by decades of use. For low-resource African languages, those conditions do not hold. The consequences are beginning to surface.

This paper presents an empirical audit of license provenance across more than twenty corpus families used in African NLP. It applies established license-compatibility rules to three case-study languages: Kituba/Munukutuba, Zarma, and Moore. It documents four structurally distinct failure modes, each supported by primary-source evidence.

Normative claim

Lawful data use is an ethical baseline, not a separate concern. For African language communities with limited institutional recourse, license violations are not only legal risks. They are ethical failures that compound existing power asymmetries.

§ 02A discipline-specific gap.

License compatibility logic and machine-readable rights expression have been standard tools in the library and open-source communities since the early 2000s. SPDX identifiers, the OSI approval process, ODRL (a W3C standard since 2001), and Creative Commons' ccREL all predate modern NLP's engagement with these issues. By two decades.

Their limited adoption in African NLP practice is therefore not due to novelty. The tools and concepts are established. The gap is discipline-specific, and documenting it is the empirical contribution of this paper.

A corpus that violates its source's license is not only a legal risk but an ethical failure toward the communities whose language it encodes.

§ 03The compatibility matrix.

The paper defines six license tiers (shown above) and a pairwise compatibility matrix describing which output license is valid when two sources are combined. Three results are practically important:

  • T3 × T4a is incompatible. Wikipedia (CC-BY-SA) and the 27Group Feriji Zarma corpus (CC-BY-NC) cannot be combined in a single published dataset under any valid license.
  • T4b × anything is blocked. Any corpus with a NoDerivs clause cannot be used to create a published annotation dataset, regardless of the output license chosen.
  • T3 and T4a propagate. Practitioners using Wikipedia as an annotation seed must release under CC-BY-SA 4.0. These are design decisions that must be made before annotation begins.

§ 04Four failure modes.

The four cases below are not a random sample of poor practice. They represent four structurally distinct ways compliance can fail, each with different causes and implications. In each case, an implicit legal assumption was made that would not have survived explicit examination.

A · Prohibition

JW300

Parallel corpus built in breach of the source's Terms of Service. A CIPIT Nairobi audit confirmed the violation; OPUS removed the corpus. Downstream models may still carry contaminated provenance.

B · Label misrepresentation

WAXAL

A 2026 speech dataset published with a uniform CC-BY 4.0 claim that contradicts per-provider terms. Lingala, Hausa, Igbo, Yoruba, and Makerere-sourced languages require CC-BY-SA output.

C · Hidden restriction

Tanzil

Quran translation corpus labelled CC-BY 3.0. The license page explicitly states the text may not be modified, a NoDerivs clause not reflected in the CC label visible to aggregators.

D · Infrastructure failure

Congolese Radio Corpus

405 YouTube IDs referenced; an audit in Feb 2026 found 402 no longer accessible. The reproducible portion is about 14.4 hours, versus the hundreds originally claimed.

§ 05A due-diligence procedure.

The paper proposes a minimal four-step workflow for license-compliant dataset construction. First, inventory sources against known-safe and known-prohibited lists. Second, assign tiers by verifying against primary license pages, not dataset cards. Third, apply the compatibility matrix. Fourth, verify ISO codes, archive snapshots to persistent repositories, and include a provenance declaration in the paper's ethics statement.

The matrix requires no legal expertise: it is a lookup table. Tier assignments require a one-time provenance check per source. The checklist is a matter of discipline.

§ 06Proposed ethics-statement language.

The NLP community has established ethics statements for human subjects and consent. But not yet for data provenance. For low-resource African languages, where a single corpus loss is unrecoverable, documentation is an ethical necessity. The paper proposes a short, standard declaration, analogous to an IRB approval statement:

All corpus sources were reviewed for provenance. No ToS-violating or prohibited sources are included. Tier assignments are documented in the supplementary material.

Margin notes

Kituba (ktu/mkw) is the vehicular language of southern Congo-Brazzaville, 5 to 8M speakers, absent from FLORES-200.

Zarma (dje) is spoken by about 5M in Niger; OCHA lists it as a priority communication language.

Moore (mos) has FLORES-200 coverage and active Burkinabè research groups, the upper bound for a relatively well-studied low-resource language.

A notable finding: the Kongo Wikipedia (kgwiki) is in fact written in Kituba; 1,200+ articles potentially overlooked by researchers searching under standard ISO codes.
