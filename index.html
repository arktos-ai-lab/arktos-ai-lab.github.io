Arktos AI Lab is an independent research lab in the Netherlands, run by one researcher who works with Claude, Anthropic's AI, as a research partner. It works at the intersection of machine learning data provenance, low-resource languages and jurisdictions, and law and economics.

Data Provenance Low-Resource NLP Law & Economics
§ 01 · Premise

One researcher, working with Claude.

Arktos AI Lab was founded in 2025 by Ernst van Gassen and is run by him. Where it fits, the lab works with Claude, Anthropic's AI, as a research partner, and the author checks and answers for every result. The work addresses two connected questions. How the data behind machine learning systems is collected and licensed. And what responsible practice looks like in that domain.

The easiest way to improve a model is, at present, to feed it more data. The most honest way is much harder.
§ 02 · Research Areas

Four lines of inquiry.

01 / NLP & Data EthicsACTIVE

Data provenance & license compatibility in low-resource NLP.

Who holds the rights in the text a corpus ships, and does its license say so? Audits of African-language corpora, a published due-diligence procedure, and corpora that trace each record to its publisher.

Read the RAIL paper →
02 / Legal InformaticsACCEPTED

A legal knowledge graph for a low-resource jurisdiction.

LexSR consolidates the legislation of Suriname and links it to case law. Accepted at JURIX 2026; the corpus of government-sourced legislation and court decisions is open on Zenodo.

03 / Speech & ArchivesPUBLISHED

Public-domain broadcast archives as speech data.

Auditing and packaging African speech resources from government archives, and automatic transcription of Voice of America African-language audio. Two papers published in the DHASA 2026 selected papers.

See publications →
04 / Law & EconomicsPUBLISHED

Comparables, crises and the trilemma.

A pan-European clustering method for transfer-pricing comparables, in IBFD's International Transfer Pricing Journal. And Russia's crisis responses read through the macroeconomic trilemma, in the Russian Journal of Economics, with Busola Ijaware.

An independent researcher working across two fields.

Arktos AI Lab is the research vehicle I founded in 2025. It brings my work in international tax and economics into conversation with my interest in how machine learning systems are built. Specifically, the data that trains them and the legal frameworks that govern it.

My NLP work began with an audit of Creative Commons licensing across African language corpora, published at RAIL 2026 (LREC). It has since grown into building corpora and legal resources: a legal knowledge graph for Suriname, accepted at JURIX 2026, and three papers at DHASA 2026 on speech archives and rights disclosure. On the economics side I have written on transfer-pricing comparables in IBFD's International Transfer Pricing Journal and, with Busola Ijaware, on Russia's crisis responses in the Russian Journal of Economics.

The lab is one researcher, working with Claude as a research partner where it fits. Claude helps with code, analysis and drafting. I choose the questions, check every result, and answer for every claim.

I welcome co-authors. If you work on data provenance, low-resource NLP, or the law and economics of AI, and you're looking for a collaborator on a paper, I'd like to hear from you. The same goes for reviewers and funders.
